## Where every session begins

Every session of Durga Puja worship — Saptami morning, Ashtami morning,
Nabami morning, sandhi puja — begins the same way. Before a single flower is
offered, the *purohit* (পুরোহিত) closes his eyes and recites the **dhyana**
(ধ্যান) mantra: a long Sanskrit verse that builds the image of the goddess
detail by detail in the mind, from the crown of matted hair down to the toe
resting on the buffalo demon. Only after the deity has been "seen" inwardly
is she worshipped outwardly. The dhyana is the priest's work; the
congregation usually hears it as a murmured Sanskrit stream while the *dhak*
(ঢাক) falls silent.

The **pranam** (প্রণাম) mantras are the opposite: they belong to everyone. A
pranam mantra is what you say when you fold your hands or touch your head to
the ground before the *murti* (মূর্তি) — at the end of *anjali* (অঞ্জলি),
when you first arrive at the pandal, or quietly on your way out. The most
famous of them, *Sarva-mangala-mangalye*, is probably the single most widely
known Sanskrit verse among Bengalis; it closes the pushpanjali every year,
and elders often say it under their breath at any shrine, not just Durga's.

Between these two poles sit the short **nama** (নাম) and **beeja** (বীজ)
mantras — compact formulas built around the goddess's name and
seed-syllable — and the great "Ya devi sarvabhuteshu" refrain from the
*Chandi* (চণ্ডী), which many people use as a pranam verse in its own right.

Throughout this chapter, and its companion, every mantra is printed the same
way: Devanagari first, then Bengali script, then a roman transliteration
with the long vowels marked, and finally the meaning in simple words. The
mantras of the anjali itself — the Vishnu-smaran that opens the rite, the
*ete gandhapushpe* flower-greetings, and the day-wise rounds — are in the
[Pushpanjali chapter](15-mantras-pushpanjali.md).

## The Durga dhyana mantra

This is the classical dhyana of the ten-armed *Mahishasuramardini*
(মহিষাসুরমর্দিনী) — the form worshipped at Durga Puja. It is found in the
Bengali priestly manuals (*paddhati*, পদ্ধতি) and runs to thirteen verses.
The priest recites it holding a flower, visualising each detail as it is
named.

> ॐ जटाजूटसमायुक्तामर्द्धेन्दुकृतशेखराम् ।
> लोचनत्रयसंयुक्तां पूर्णेन्दुसदृशाननाम् ॥१॥
> अतसीपुष्पवर्णाभां सुप्रतिष्ठां सुलोचनाम् ।
> नवयौवनसम्पन्नां सर्वाभरणभूषिताम् ॥२॥
> सुचारुदशनां तद्वत् पीनोन्नतपयोधराम् ।
> त्रिभङ्गस्थानसंस्थानां महिषासुरमर्दिनीम् ॥३॥
> मृणालायतसंस्पर्शदशबाहुसमन्विताम् ।
> त्रिशूलं दक्षिणे ध्येयं खड्गं चक्रं क्रमादधः ॥४॥
> तीक्ष्णबाणं तथा शक्तिं दक्षिणे सन्निवेशयेत् ।
> खेटकं पूर्णचापञ्च पाशमङ्कुशमेव च ॥५॥
> घण्टां वा परशुं वापि वामतः सन्निवेशयेत् ।
> अधस्तान्महिषं तद्वद्विशिरस्कं प्रदर्शयेत् ॥६॥
> शिरश्छेदोद्भवं तद्वद्दानवं खड्गपाणिनम् ।
> हृदि शूलेन निर्भिन्नं निर्यदन्त्रविभूषितम् ॥७॥
> रक्तरक्तीकृताङ्गञ्च रक्तविस्फुरितेक्षणम् ।
> वेष्टितं नागपाशेन भ्रुकुटीभीषणाननम् ॥८॥
> सपाशवामहस्तेन धृतकेशञ्च दुर्गया ।
> वमद्रुधिरवक्त्रञ्च देव्याः सिंहं प्रदर्शयेत् ॥९॥
> देव्यास्तु दक्षिणं पादं समं सिंहोपरिस्थितम् ।
> किञ्चिदूर्द्ध्वं तथा वाममङ्गुष्ठं महिषोपरि ॥१०॥
> शत्रुक्षयकरीं देवीं दैत्यदानवदर्पहाम् ।
> प्रसन्नवदनां देवीं सर्वकामफलप्रदाम् ।
> स्तूयमानञ्च तद्रूपममरैः सन्निवेशयेत् ॥११॥
> उग्रचण्डा प्रचण्डा च चण्डोग्रा चण्डनायिका ।
> चण्डा चण्डवती चैव चण्डरूपातिचण्डिका ॥१२॥
> आभिः शक्तिभिरष्टाभिः सततं परिवेष्टिताम् ।
> चिन्तयेत् सततं दुर्गां धर्मकामार्थमोक्षदाम् ॥१३॥
>
> ওঁ জটাজূটসমাযুক্তামর্দ্ধেন্দুকৃতশেখরাম্।
> লোচনত্রয়সংযুক্তাং পূর্ণেন্দুসদৃশাননাম্॥১॥
> অতসীপুষ্পবর্ণাভাং সুপ্রতিষ্ঠাং সুলোচনাম্।
> নবযৌবনসম্পন্নাং সর্বাভরণভূষিতাম্॥২॥
> সুচারুদশনাং তদ্বৎ পীনোন্নতপয়োধরাম্।
> ত্রিভঙ্গস্থানসংস্থানাং মহিষাসুরমর্দিনীম্॥৩॥
> মৃণালায়তসংস্পর্শদশবাহুসমন্বিতাম্।
> ত্রিশূলং দক্ষিণে ধ্যেয়ং খড়্গং চক্রং ক্রমাদধঃ॥৪॥
> তীক্ষ্ণবাণং তথা শক্তিং দক্ষিণে সন্নিবেশয়েৎ।
> খেটকং পূর্ণচাপঞ্চ পাশমঙ্কুশমেব চ॥৫॥
> ঘণ্টাং বা পরশুং বাপি বামতঃ সন্নিবেশয়েৎ।
> অধস্তান্মহিষং তদ্বদ্বিশিরস্কং প্রদর্শয়েৎ॥৬॥
> শিরশ্ছেদোদ্ভবং তদ্বদ্দানবং খড়্গপাণিনম্।
> হৃদি শূলেন নির্ভিন্নং নির্যদন্ত্রবিভূষিতম্॥৭॥
> রক্তরক্তীকৃতাঙ্গঞ্চ রক্তবিস্ফুরিতেক্ষণম্।
> বেষ্টিতং নাগপাশেন ভ্রুকুটীভীষণাননম্॥৮॥
> সপাশবামহস্তেন ধৃতকেশঞ্চ দুর্গয়া।
> বমদ্রুধিরবক্ত্রঞ্চ দেব্যাঃ সিংহং প্রদর্শয়েৎ॥৯॥
> দেব্যাস্তু দক্ষিণং পাদং সমং সিংহোপরিস্থিতম্।
> কিঞ্চিদূর্দ্ধ্বং তথা বামমঙ্গুষ্ঠং মহিষোপরি॥১০॥
> শত্রুক্ষয়করীং দেবীং দৈত্যদানবদর্পহাম্।
> প্রসন্নবদনাং দেবীং সর্বকামফলপ্রদাম্।
> স্তূয়মানঞ্চ তদ্রূপমমরৈঃ সন্নিবেশয়েৎ॥১১॥
> উগ্রচণ্ডা প্রচণ্ডা চ চণ্ডোগ্রা চণ্ডনায়িকা।
> চণ্ডা চণ্ডবতী চৈব চণ্ডরূপাতিচণ্ডিকা॥১২॥
> আভিঃ শক্তিভিরষ্টাভিঃ সততং পরিবেষ্টিতাম্।
> চিন্তয়েৎ সততং দুর্গাং ধর্মকামার্থমোক্ষদাম্॥১৩॥
>
> *oṁ jaṭājūṭa-samāyuktām arddhendu-kṛta-śekharām,
> locana-traya-saṁyuktāṁ pūrṇendu-sadṛśānanām. (1)
> atasī-puṣpa-varṇābhāṁ supratiṣṭhāṁ sulocanām,
> nava-yauvana-sampannāṁ sarvābharaṇa-bhūṣitām. (2)
> sucāru-daśanāṁ tadvat pīnonnata-payodharām,
> tribhaṅga-sthāna-saṁsthānāṁ mahiṣāsura-mardinīm. (3)
> mṛṇālāyata-saṁsparśa-daśa-bāhu-samanvitām,
> triśūlaṁ dakṣiṇe dhyeyaṁ khaḍgaṁ cakraṁ kramād adhaḥ. (4)
> tīkṣṇa-bāṇaṁ tathā śaktiṁ dakṣiṇe sanniveśayet,
> kheṭakaṁ pūrṇa-cāpañ ca pāśam aṅkuśam eva ca. (5)
> ghaṇṭāṁ vā paraśuṁ vāpi vāmataḥ sanniveśayet,
> adhastān mahiṣaṁ tadvad viśiraskaṁ pradarśayet. (6)
> śiraś-chedodbhavaṁ tadvad dānavaṁ khaḍga-pāṇinam,
> hṛdi śūlena nirbhinnaṁ niryad-antra-vibhūṣitam. (7)
> rakta-raktī-kṛtāṅgañ ca rakta-visphuritekṣaṇam,
> veṣṭitaṁ nāga-pāśena bhrukuṭī-bhīṣaṇānanam. (8)
> sapāśa-vāma-hastena dhṛta-keśañ ca durgayā,
> vamad-rudhira-vaktrañ ca devyāḥ siṁhaṁ pradarśayet. (9)
> devyās tu dakṣiṇaṁ pādaṁ samaṁ siṁhopari-sthitam,
> kiñcid ūrddhvaṁ tathā vāmam aṅguṣṭhaṁ mahiṣopari. (10)
> śatru-kṣaya-karīṁ devīṁ daitya-dānava-darpahām,
> prasanna-vadanāṁ devīṁ sarva-kāma-phala-pradām,
> stūyamānañ ca tad-rūpam amaraiḥ sanniveśayet. (11)
> ugracaṇḍā pracaṇḍā ca caṇḍogrā caṇḍa-nāyikā,
> caṇḍā caṇḍavatī caiva caṇḍa-rūpāticaṇḍikā. (12)
> ābhiḥ śaktibhir aṣṭābhiḥ satataṁ pariveṣṭitām,
> cintayet satataṁ durgāṁ dharma-kāmārtha-mokṣadām. (13)*

**In simple words:** The verse is a painter's brief. Meditate, it says, on
the goddess whose piled, matted hair is crowned by the half-moon; who has
three eyes and a face like the full moon; whose skin glows the colour of the
atasi (flax) flower; who stands firm and lovely, bright-eyed, in the
fullness of youth, adorned with every ornament — beautiful teeth, full form,
her body flexed in the graceful *tribhanga* (three-bend) stance even as she
crushes Mahishasura. Her ten arms are long and soft as lotus stalks. Then it
arms her, hand by hand: in the right hands visualise the trident, and below
it in order the sword, the discus, the sharp arrow and the spear; on the
left the shield, the fully drawn bow, the noose, the goad, and the bell or
the battle-axe. Beneath her place the buffalo, its head struck off, and
rising from the severed neck the demon in his true form, sword still in
hand — pierced through the heart by her trident, entrails spilling, his body
red with blood, eyes blazing, bound in the serpent-noose, his face terrible
with knitted brows, his hair gripped in the goddess's noose-bearing left
hand. Show her lion, its mouth streaming with the blood of the kill. Her
right foot rests flat on the lion; the toe of her slightly raised left foot
presses on the buffalo. She destroys enemies and breaks the pride of
demons, yet her own face is serene, granting the fruit of every wish, while
the gods stand around her singing praise. Encircling her stand her eight
*shakti* attendants — Ugrachanda, Prachanda, Chandogra, Chandanayika,
Chanda, Chandavati, Chandarupa and Atichandika. On her, giver of dharma,
desire, wealth and liberation, one should meditate constantly.

Everything you see in the pandal — the number of arms, which weapon sits in
which hand, the lion mid-bite, the demon emerging from the buffalo's neck,
the serene face above the violence — comes from this verse. The *murti* is
this mantra in clay: the artisan and the Sanskrit describe each other, line
by line, and reading the dhyana beside a photograph of any traditional
protima is the best commentary either will ever get.

**Notes**

- **Who recites it:** the purohit alone, at the start of each day's puja,
  before the offerings begin. Devotees are not expected to know it, though
  hearing it with the translation in mind transforms how you see the image.
- **Text variants:** the manuals differ in small ways. For the goddess's
  complexion, sources split between *atasī-puṣpa-varṇābhāṁ* ("lustrous as
  the flax flower") and the reading common in Bengali paddhati,
  *tapta-kāñcana-varṇābhāṁ* ("glowing like molten gold") — the second
  matches the golden-complexioned Bengali Durga. In verse 4 some texts read
  *dakṣiṇe dhyeyaṁ* ("to be visualised in the right hand") and others
  *dakṣiṇe pāṇau* ("in the right hand"). In the final verse some read
  *cintayet satataṁ durgāṁ* and others *cintayej jagatāṁ dhātrīṁ*
  ("meditate on the sustainer of the worlds"). None of these change the
  meaning.
- **A different dhyana elsewhere:** North Indian sources often print a
  shorter dhyana that opens similarly but continues differently
  (*ardhendu-kṛta-lakṣaṇām … padmendu-sadyaśānanām*). If you compare texts
  across websites, that is why the openings don't quite match.
- **Pronunciation:** the frequent verb *sanniveśayet* ("one should place")
  ends each weapon-list line — it is an instruction to the meditator, not an
  epithet of the goddess.

## The Durga pranam mantra — Sarva-mangala-mangalye

The universal salutation verse, recited with folded hands or a full bow. It
comes from the *Narayani Stuti* — the hymn the gods sing to the goddess in
chapter 11 of the *Devi Mahatmya* (the Chandi), just after she has slain the
demons Shumbha and Nishumbha.

> ॐ सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके ।
> शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> ওঁ সর্বমঙ্গলমাঙ্গল্যে শিবে সর্বার্থসাধিকে।
> শরণ্যে ত্র্যম্বকে গৌরি নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *oṁ sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye śive sarvārtha-sādhike,
> śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo'stu te.*

**In simple words:** O auspiciousness within everything auspicious; O
gracious one; you who accomplish every aim; O refuge; three-eyed one; fair
Gauri; Narayani — salutation to you. It is a single breath of surrender:
whatever is good anywhere, she is the goodness in it; whatever needs doing,
she is the power that gets it done; and when there is nowhere else to go,
she is where you go.

**The epithets, one by one**

- *sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye* — "the auspiciousness of all auspicious things":
  not merely one good thing among many, but the goodness inside every good
  thing.
- *śive* — "O gracious/benign one"; also the feminine of Shiva, hinting at
  her identity as Shiva's consort and his power.
- *sarvārtha-sādhike* — "accomplisher of all aims" (*artha* covers purpose,
  wealth and goal alike).
- *śaraṇye* — "worthy of refuge; the one in whom shelter is taken."
- *tryambake* — "three-eyed one," the feminine counterpart of Shiva's title
  Tryambaka.
- *gauri* — "the fair, radiant one," Parvati's own name.
- *nārāyaṇi* — the goddess as the shakti of Narayana — the supreme power
  seen as feminine.
- *namo'stu te* — "let there be salutation to you."

**Notes**

- **Who recites it:** everyone. It is the closing pranam of the Saptami
  anjali in our day-wise sequence — see the
  [Pushpanjali chapter](15-mantras-pushpanjali.md) — and the verse people
  instinctively reach for at any pranam, on any day.
- **Reading variant:** scriptural editions of the Devi Mahatmya read
  *māṅgalye* (माङ्गल्ये, long ā); many puja compilations and printed anjali
  cards simplify it to *maṅgalye* — our samiti's own anjali texts do. In
  recitation the difference is barely audible; the Chandi reading is
  *māṅgalye*.
- **Its neighbours:** in the Chandi this verse sits in a chain of
  salutations all ending *nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*; two of them
  (*sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśānāṁ…* and *śaraṇāgata-dīnārta…*) are the second and
  third rounds of the Mahashtami anjali.

## Jayanti mangala Kali — the litany of her names

The second pranam every Bengali is expected to carry by heart. It is the
opening verse of the *Argala Stotram*, recited before every Chandipath, and
in our day-wise anjali sequence it is the Mahashtami pranam.

> ॐ जयन्ती मङ्गला काली भद्रकाली कपालिनी ।
> दुर्गा शिवा क्षमा धात्री स्वाहा स्वधा नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> ওঁ জয়ন্তী মঙ্গলা কালী ভদ্রকালী কপালিনী।
> দুর্গা শিবা ক্ষমা ধাত্রী স্বাহা স্বধা নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *oṁ jayantī maṅgalā kālī bhadrakālī kapālinī,
> durgā śivā kṣamā dhātrī svāhā svadhā namo'stu te.*

**In simple words:** You who are Jayanti the ever-victorious, Mangala the
auspicious, Kali, Bhadrakali, Kapalini the skull-bearer; Durga, Shiva the
gracious, Kshama who is forgiveness itself, Dhatri the sustainer; Svaha and
Svadha — the very offering-calls by which oblations reach the gods and the
ancestors — salutation to you. The verse teaches, in one breath, that all
the goddesses, and even the sacred syllables of sacrifice, are one Devi.

## The short nama and beeja mantras

Alongside the long verses runs a family of very short mantras built on the
goddess's name (*nama*) and her seed-syllable (*beeja*), *duṁ*. These are
japa mantras — meant for quiet, private repetition rather than public
recitation — and they also appear inside the priest's liturgy at various
points.

> ॐ दुं दुर्गायै नमः ।
>
> ওঁ দুং দুর্গায়ৈ নমঃ।
>
> *oṁ duṁ durgāyai namaḥ.*

> ॐ ह्रीं दुं दुर्गायै नमः ।
>
> ওঁ হ্রীং দুং দুর্গায়ৈ নমঃ।
>
> *oṁ hrīṁ duṁ durgāyai namaḥ.*

> ॐ दुर्गे दुर्गे रक्षणि स्वाहा ।
>
> ওঁ দুর্গে দুর্গে রক্ষণি স্বাহা।
>
> *oṁ durge durge rakṣaṇi svāhā.*

**In simple words:** The first is the plainest possible act of homage: "Om.
*Duṁ*. Salutation to Durga." The syllable *duṁ* is treated in tantric
tradition as the sound-form of Durga herself, so name and seed together
invoke her fully. The second adds *hrīṁ*, the seed-syllable of the great
goddess Mahamaya, making the eight-syllable (*ashtakshara*) form found in
the Bengali manuals, where it is cited from the Kalika Purana tradition. The
third calls on her twice by name and as *rakṣaṇi* — "protectress" — with the
offering-word *svāhā*: "O Durga, Durga, O protectress, svaha."

**Notes**

- **Who recites them:** anyone, at any time; no priest is needed. Within the
  puja itself the purohit uses the name-and-beeja formulas when making
  individual offerings.
- **A sober word:** websites surround these mantras with elaborate promises.
  Traditionally they are simply concentrated forms of remembering the
  goddess — their value is in steady, attentive repetition, not in
  transactional claims.
- **Pronunciation:** *duṁ* rhymes with "room" (short), with a nasal hum at
  the end — not "dum" as in English. *durgāyai* is the dative: "to Durga."

## "Ya devi sarvabhuteshu" — the Chandi refrain as pranam

From chapter 5 of the *Devi Mahatmya* comes the most famous refrain in all
goddess literature. The full hymn salutes the Devi as she exists in all
beings under some two dozen aspects — as consciousness, intelligence, sleep,
hunger, shadow, thirst, forgiveness, modesty, peace, faith, loveliness,
fortune, activity, memory, compassion, contentment, mother, and more — each
aspect receiving the same thunderous triple salutation. In pranam usage, one
verse of the litany stands for the whole, and by far the most quoted is the
*shakti* verse:

> या देवी सर्वभूतेषु शक्तिरूपेण संस्थिता ।
> नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमो नमः ॥
>
> যা দেবী সর্বভূতেষু শক্তিরূপেণ সংস্থিতা।
> নমস্তস্যৈ নমস্তস্যৈ নমস্তস্যৈ নমো নমঃ॥
>
> *yā devī sarva-bhūteṣu śakti-rūpeṇa saṁsthitā,
> namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ.*

**In simple words:** To the goddess who lives in every being as power
itself — salutation to her, salutation to her, salutation to her,
salutation again and again. The point of the verse is its theology: the
goddess is not only in the pandal; she is the strength in every living
thing, and bowing to her is bowing to that. The triple *namas tasyai* is
deliberately relentless — the Sanskrit equivalent of bowing three times, and
then once more.

**Notes**

- **Who recites it:** both priest and public. The purohit chants the full
  litany during *Chandipath* (চণ্ডীপাঠ) recitations; individuals use single
  verses — most often the *śakti-rūpeṇa* and *mātṛ-rūpeṇa* ("in the form of
  mother") verses — as pranam mantras. During cultural programmes you will
  hear it sung; on Mahalaya morning it anchors the *Mahishasuramardini*
  broadcast.
- **The full litany:** the complete sequence of "Ya devi sarvabhuteshu"
  verses, with all the aspects in order, belongs to the Chandipath — see
  [The Chandi](20-mantras-the-chandi.md) for the whole hymn in context.
- **Pronunciation:** *tasyai* is two syllables, "tas-yai"; the phrase is
  *namas tasyai*, run together in recitation as "namastasyai."

## How to use this chapter

Learn the two pranam verses first — *Sarva-mangala-mangalye* and *Jayanti
mangala Kali* are the layperson's whole required repertoire, and with them
no anjali, arati or pandal visit ever leaves you silent. Keep a nama mantra
for the quiet moments. The dhyana rewards slower study: read it beside a
photograph of any traditional protima and watch the artisans and the
Sanskrit describe each other. And when the flowers are put in your hands on
Saptami morning, turn to the [Pushpanjali chapter](15-mantras-pushpanjali.md) —
that is where the liturgy becomes yours.
