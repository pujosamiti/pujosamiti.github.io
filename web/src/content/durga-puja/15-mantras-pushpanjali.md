# Pushpanjali Mantras

**Pushpanjali** (পুষ্পাঞ্জলি) — "an offering of flowers from cupped hands" —
is the one moment of the pujo when the liturgy passes from the purohit's
mouth to everyone's. Most of the year the mantras belong to the priest; at
anjali the whole community repeats them line by line, flowers in hand, and
offers them at the Devi's feet. For many of us it is the emotional centre of
each pujo day — and the part most people wish they understood word by word.

This chapter follows the texts our samiti actually uses — the day-wise
Bengali sarbojanin sequence, as printed in the Bhaktikatha and Bangla
Panjika compilations that our reference PDF reproduces. The essential thing
it preserves, which shorter guides flatten, is that **the anjali mantras are
different on each day**: Saptami's rounds ask, Mahashtami's rounds praise
the demon-slayer, Nabami's rounds contemplate the goddess as time and
dissolution. Each day has three rounds, a shared offering refrain, and its
own closing pranam. As throughout this book, every mantra is printed in
Devanagari, then Bengali script, then roman transliteration with long
vowels marked, followed by the meaning in simple words.

## How anjali works

You come bathed, traditionally fasting until the anjali is done. The purohit
distributes flowers and *belpata* (বেলপাতা — bel leaves, often touched with
sandal paste); you hold them in cupped hands at chest height. The purohit
recites each line and pauses; everyone repeats. At the end of each round he
says the offering refrain and everyone throws the flowers toward the Devi's
feet — then takes fresh flowers for the next round. Three rounds, then the
day's *pranam mantra* (প্রণাম মন্ত্র) with folded or raised hands, often
followed by *shanti jal* (শান্তি জল) — peace-water sprinkled over bowed
heads with a mango sprig or durba bundle — and a sip of *charanamrita*
(চরণামৃত), the water that has washed the Devi's feet, taken from the right
palm. Then the fast is broken. If you miss a word, tradition is untroubled:
the offering is the attention, not the elocution.

A few practicalities. Hold the flowers so the bel leaf lies on top — it is
the Devi's leaf. You offer facing the protima but from wherever you stand;
the crowd at Ashtami anjali (see [Ashtami](09-ashtami.md)) is itself part of
the rite. Children join fully — a mantra repeated imperfectly in a hundred
small voices is precisely how this liturgy has been transmitted for
centuries. And anyone who cannot fast — the ill, the elderly, the
pregnant — is explicitly excused by every tradition; the goddess, priests
like to say, is a mother before she is a queen.

## The opening — Vishnu smaran and purification

Bengali puja convention opens every rite by remembering Vishnu, whose name
purifies the speaker in whatever state they arrive:

> नमः विष्णुः नमः विष्णुः नमः विष्णुः ।
> ॐ अपवित्रः पवित्रो वा सर्वावस्थां गतोऽपि वा ।
> यः स्मरेत् पुण्डरीकाक्षं स बाह्याभ्यन्तरः शुचिः ॥
> नमः सर्वमङ्गलमङ्गल्यां वरेण्यं वरदं शुभम् ।
> नारायणं नमस्कृत्य सर्वकर्माणि कारयेत् ॥
>
> নমঃ বিষ্ণুঃ নমঃ বিষ্ণুঃ নমঃ বিষ্ণুঃ।
> ওঁ অপবিত্রঃ পবিত্রো বা সর্বাবস্থাং গতোঽপি বা।
> যঃ স্মরেৎ পুণ্ডরীকাক্ষং স বাহ্যাভ্যন্তরঃ শুচিঃ॥
> নমঃ সর্বমঙ্গলমঙ্গল্যাং বরেণ্যং বরদং শুভম্।
> নারায়ণং নমস্কৃত্য সর্বকর্মাণি কারয়েৎ॥
>
> *namaḥ viṣṇuḥ namaḥ viṣṇuḥ namaḥ viṣṇuḥ ·
> oṁ apavitraḥ pavitro vā sarvāvasthāṁ gato'pi vā ·
> yaḥ smaret puṇḍarīkākṣaṁ sa bāhyābhyantaraḥ śuciḥ ·
> namaḥ sarvamaṅgala-maṅgalyāṁ vareṇyaṁ varadaṁ śubham ·
> nārāyaṇaṁ namaskṛtya sarva-karmāṇi kārayet*

**In simple words:** Vishnu, Vishnu, Vishnu. Whether one is pure or impure,
in whatever condition one has come — whoever remembers the lotus-eyed Lord
becomes clean, outside and within. Having bowed to Narayana — auspicious
among all that is auspicious, worthy of worship, the giver of blessings —
one may begin all rites. It is the liturgy's generous front door: nobody is
too unprepared to offer a flower.

*(Some compilations open with "oṁ viṣṇuḥ oṁ viṣṇuḥ oṁ viṣṇuḥ" rather than
"namaḥ viṣṇuḥ…"; our samiti's printed sources use the namaḥ form. The sense
is identical.)*

## Ete gandhapushpe — flowers for every seat

Before the Devi receives her anjali, scented flowers are offered to the
other seats of the pandal, each with the same simple formula, *ete
gandhapuṣpe … namaḥ* — "with these fragrant flowers, salutation to —":

> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ नारायणाय नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ श्रीगुरवे नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ श्रीगणेशाय नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ दुर्गायै नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ शिवाय नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ लक्ष्मीदेव्यै नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ सरस्वत्यै नमः ।
> एते सुगन्धिपुष्पे ॐ कार्तिकाय नमः ।
> एते गन्धपुष्पे ॐ सर्वदेवदेवीभ्यो नमः ॥
>
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ নারায়ণায় নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ শ্রীগুরবে নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ শ্রীগণেশায় নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ দুর্গায়ৈ নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ শিবায় নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ লক্ষ্মীদেব্যৈ নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ সরস্বত্যৈ নমঃ।
> এতে সুগন্ধিপুষ্পে ওঁ কার্তিকায় নমঃ।
> এতে গন্ধপুষ্পে ওঁ সর্বদেবদেবীভ্যো নমঃ॥
>
> *ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ nārāyaṇāya namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ śrī-gurave namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ śrī-gaṇeśāya namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ durgāyai namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ śivāya namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ lakṣmī-devyai namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ sarasvatyai namaḥ ·
> ete sugandhi-puṣpe oṁ kārtikāya namaḥ ·
> ete gandhapuṣpe oṁ sarva-devadevībhyo namaḥ*

**In simple words:** With these fragrant flowers — salutation to Narayana;
to the revered guru; to Ganesha; to Durga; to Shiva; to the goddess
Lakshmi; to Saraswati; to Kartik; and finally to all gods and goddesses
together — so no seat in the ek-chala frame goes ungreeted.

**A note on the Kartik line:** both our printed sources (the Bhaktikatha PDF
and its web page) render this line in a corrupted form — "ওমে সুগন্ধি ফুল,
ওমে কার্তিকা" — evidently a typesetting degradation in the compilation
itself, not a different mantra. The reading given above, *ete
sugandhi-puṣpe oṁ kārtikāya namaḥ* ("with these fragrant flowers,
salutation to Kartika"), restores it by the formula every neighbouring line
follows, with *sugandhi* — "fragrant" — accounting for the "সুগন্ধি ফুল"
fragment the sources preserve. Worth confirming once with our purohit
mashai.

## Saptami anjali — the asking day

### Round 1

> नमः आयुर्देहि यशो देहि भाग्यं भगवति देहि मे ।
> पुत्रान् देहि धनं देहि सर्वान् कामांश्च देहि मे ॥
>
> নমঃ আয়ুর্দেহি যশো দেহি ভাগ্যং ভগবতি দেহি মে।
> পুত্রান্ দেহি ধনং দেহি সর্বান্ কামাংশ্চ দেহি মে॥
>
> *namaḥ āyur dehi yaśo dehi bhāgyaṁ bhagavati dehi me ·
> putrān dehi dhanaṁ dehi sarvān kāmāṁś ca dehi me*

**In simple words:** Give me long life, Bhagavati — give me honour, give me
good fortune; give me children, give me the means to live, give me all that
I long for. The first round is unabashed asking: the Devi is the mother to
whom one may simply hold out a hand.

### Round 2

> हर पापं हर क्लेशं हर शोकं हरासुखम् ।
> हर रोगं हर क्षोभं हर मारीं हरप्रिये ॥
>
> হর পাপং হর ক্লেশং হর শোকং হরাসুখম্।
> হর রোগং হর ক্ষোভং হর মারীং হরপ্রিয়ে॥
>
> *hara pāpaṁ hara kleśaṁ hara śokaṁ harāsukham ·
> hara rogaṁ hara kṣobhaṁ hara mārīṁ hara-priye*

**In simple words:** Take away sin, take away affliction, take away grief
and misery; take away disease, turmoil and pestilence, O beloved of Hara
(Shiva). Where the first round asks for what we lack, the second asks to be
relieved of what we carry — the verse is a drumbeat of *hara, hara, hara*:
take it, take it, take it.

### Round 3

> संग्रामे विजयं देहि धनं देहि सदा गृहे ।
> धर्मार्थकामसम्पत्तिं देहि देवी नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> সংগ্রামে বিজয়ং দেহি ধনং দেহি সদা গৃহে।
> ধর্মার্থকামসম্পত্তিং দেহি দেবী নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *saṁgrāme vijayaṁ dehi dhanaṁ dehi sadā gṛhe ·
> dharmārtha-kāma-sampattiṁ dehi devī namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** Grant victory in every struggle, prosperity always at
home, and the full wealth of dharma, artha and kama — righteousness, means
and joy — salutation to you, Devi.

### The offering refrain (recited as the flowers are thrown)

> एष सचन्दन-पुष्पबिल्वपत्राञ्जलिः
> ॐ नमः दक्षयज्ञविनाशिन्यै महाघोरायै
> योगिनीकोटिपरिवृतायै भद्रकाल्यै भगवत्यै दुर्गायै नमः ॥
>
> এষ সচন্দন-পুষ্পবিল্বপত্রাঞ্জলিঃ
> ওঁ নমঃ দক্ষযজ্ঞবিনাশিন্যৈ মহাঘোরায়ৈ
> যোগিনীকোটিপরিবৃতায়ৈ ভদ্রকাল্যৈ ভগবত্যৈ দুর্গায়ৈ নমঃ॥
>
> *eṣa sacandana-puṣpa-bilvapatrāñjaliḥ ·
> oṁ namaḥ dakṣayajña-vināśinyai mahāghorāyai
> yoginī-koṭi-parivṛtāyai bhadrakālyai bhagavatyai durgāyai namaḥ*

**In simple words:** *This* — this handful of flowers and bel leaves touched
with sandal — to the destroyer of Daksha's sacrifice, the immensely awesome
one, she who moves surrounded by ten million yoginis: Bhadrakali, Bhagavati,
Durga, salutation. This is the line under which the flowers actually leave
your hands; it names the anjali itself (*eṣa … añjaliḥ*, "this
offering-handful") and gives it an address.

### Saptami pranam

> सर्वमङ्गलमङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके ।
> शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तुते ॥
>
> সর্বমঙ্গলমঙ্গল্যে শিবে সর্বার্থসাধিকে।
> শরণ্যে ত্র্যম্বকে গৌরি নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তুতে॥
>
> *sarva-maṅgala-maṅgalye śive sarvārtha-sādhike ·
> śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** O auspiciousness of all that is auspicious, gracious
one, accomplisher of every aim, refuge, three-eyed Gauri, Narayani —
salutation to you. This most beloved of all Devi verses comes from the
Narayani Stuti (Devi Mahatmya, chapter 11); its epithets are unpacked one by
one in [Dhyana and Pranam Mantras](14-mantras-dhyana-and-pranam.md), and the
hymn it comes from in [The Chandi](20-mantras-the-chandi.md).

## Mahashtami anjali — the slayer praised

For the day itself — sandhi puja, kumari puja, the year's biggest crowd —
see [Ashtami](09-ashtami.md).

### Round 1

> नमः महिषघ्नि महामाये चामुण्डे मुण्डमालिनि ।
> आयुरारोग्यविजयं देहि देवी नमोऽस्तुते ॥
>
> নমঃ মহিষঘ্নি মহামায়ে চামুণ্ডে মুণ্ডমালিনি।
> আয়ুরারোগ্যবিজয়ং দেহি দেবী নমোঽস্তুতে॥
>
> *namaḥ mahiṣaghni mahāmāye cāmuṇḍe muṇḍamālini ·
> āyur-ārogya-vijayaṁ dehi devī namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** Salutation, slayer of Mahisha, great Maya, Chamunda
garlanded with severed heads — grant long life, health and victory. On the
festival's fiercest day, the first breath of the anjali names her fiercest
forms.

### Round 2

> नमः सृष्टिस्थितिविनाशानां शक्तिभूते सनातनि ।
> गुणाश्रये गुणमये नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> নমঃ সৃষ্টিস্থিতিবিনাশানাং শক্তিভূতে সনাতনি।
> গুণাশ্রয়ে গুণময়ে নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *namaḥ sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśānāṁ śakti-bhūte sanātani ·
> guṇāśraye guṇamaye nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** You who are the power behind creation, preservation and
dissolution, eternal one; the resort of all qualities and made of them —
Narayani, salutation. (This is Devi Mahatmya 11.11, from the Narayani
Stuti.)

### Round 3

> नमः शरणागतदीनार्तपरित्राणपरायणे ।
> सर्वस्यार्तिहरे देवी नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> নমঃ শরণাগতদীনার্তপরিত্রাণপরায়ণে।
> সর্বস্যার্তিহরে দেবী নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *namaḥ śaraṇāgata-dīnārta-paritrāṇa-parāyaṇe ·
> sarvasyārti-hare devī nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** You whose very nature is to rescue the poor and the
distressed who come to you for shelter, remover of everyone's pain —
Narayani, salutation. (Devi Mahatmya 11.12.) That anyone at all may come,
and that coming is enough — this verse is the theology of anjali in one
line.

*The offering refrain — एष सचन्दन-पुष्पबिल्वपत्राञ्जलिः… / এষ
সচন্দন-পুষ্পবিল্বপত্রাঞ্জলিঃ… — is recited as on Saptami.*

### Mahashtami pranam

> जयन्ती मङ्गला काली भद्रकाली कपालिनी ।
> दुर्गा शिवा क्षमा धात्री स्वाहा स्वधा नमोऽस्तुते ॥
>
> জয়ন্তী মঙ্গলা কালী ভদ্রকালী কপালিনী।
> দুর্গা শিবা ক্ষমা ধাত্রী স্বাহা স্বধা নমোঽস্তুতে॥
>
> *jayantī maṅgalā kālī bhadrakālī kapālinī ·
> durgā śivā kṣamā dhātrī svāhā svadhā namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** A garland of her names — the ever-victorious, the
auspicious, Kali, Bhadrakali, the skull-bearer; Durga, the gracious,
forgiveness itself, the sustainer; *svaha* and *svadha*, the very words by
which offerings reach gods and ancestors — salutation to you. This is the
opening verse of the Argala Stotram, recited before every Chandipath; on
Mahashtami it becomes everyone's pranam. (It is glossed word by word in
[Dhyana and Pranam Mantras](14-mantras-dhyana-and-pranam.md).)

## Nabami anjali — the contemplation

### Round 1

> कालि कालि महाकालि कालिके कालरात्रिके ।
> धर्मकामप्रदे देवि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> কালি কালি মহাকালি কালিকে কালরাত্রিকে।
> ধর্মকামপ্রদে দেবি নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *kāli kāli mahākāli kālike kālarātrike ·
> dharma-kāma-prade devi nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** Kali, Kali, great Kali, Kalika, night-of-time — and yet
giver of dharma and of the heart's desire — Narayani, salutation. The verse
holds her two faces in one breath: the devourer of time, and the mother who
gives.

### Round 2

> लक्ष्मि लज्जे महाविद्ये श्रद्धे पुष्टि स्वधे ध्रुवे ।
> महारात्रि महामाये नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> লক্ষ্মি লজ্জে মহাবিদ্যে শ্রদ্ধে পুষ্টি স্বধে ধ্রুবে।
> মহারাত্রি মহামায়ে নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *lakṣmi lajje mahāvidye śraddhe puṣṭi svadhe dhruve ·
> mahārātri mahāmāye nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** You are Lakshmi and modesty, the great knowledge and
faith, nourishment, svadha, the fixed pole-star; the great night and the
great Maya — Narayani, salutation. She is named here as the inner quality of
everything good — the litany form the Chandi loves; compare the "Ya devi
sarvabhuteshu" litany in [The Chandi](20-mantras-the-chandi.md).

### Round 3

> कलाकाष्ठादिरूपेण परिणामप्रदायिनि ।
> विश्वस्योपरतौ शक्ते नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
>
> কলাকাষ্ঠাদিরূপেণ পরিণামপ্রদায়িনি।
> বিশ্বস্যোপরতৌ শক্তে নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
>
> *kalā-kāṣṭhādi-rūpeṇa pariṇāma-pradāyini ·
> viśvasyoparatau śakte nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** In the form of the divisions of time — the *kala*, the
*kashtha*, the instants — you are the bringer of all change; and you are the
power in the world's coming to rest. On the last full day of worship, with
farewell already in the air, the community recites a verse about time and
endings. The placement is not an accident.

*The offering refrain — এষ সচন্দন-পুষ্পবিল্বপত্রাঞ্জলিঃ… — is recited as on
the other days.*

### Nabami pranam

> सर्वस्वरूपे सर्वेशे सर्वशक्तिसमन्विते ।
> भयेभ्यस्त्राहि नो देवि दुर्गे देवि नमोऽस्तुते ॥
>
> সর্বস্বরূপে সর্বেশে সর্বশক্তিসমন্বিতে।
> ভয়েভ্যস্ত্রাহি নো দেবি দুর্গে দেবি নমোঽস্তুতে॥
>
> *sarvasvarūpe sarveśe sarvaśakti-samanvite ·
> bhayebhyas trāhi no devi durge devi namo'stu te*

**In simple words:** You whose form is everything, sovereign of all, joined
with every power — save us from our fears, Durga; Devi, salutation to you.
Of all the pranams, the plainest ask: *bhayebhyas trāhi naḥ* — from the
fears, rescue us.

## Notes

- **Sources.** This chapter follows the day-wise Bengali sarbojanin texts as
  printed in the Bhaktikatha compilation (whose PDF our samiti keeps) and
  the Bangla Panjika pushpanjali page, which agree with each other; several
  of the rounds are identifiable verses of the Devi Mahatmya's Narayani
  Stuti (chapter 11) and the Argala Stotram, noted above where the
  identification is certain.
- **Your purohit's sequence wins.** Compilations differ in small ways — the
  order of rounds, an epithet's form (*hara-priye* vs *hari-priye* appears
  in print both ways), whether the refrain is said once or after every
  round. Many pandals also use a single trio of rounds on all three days —
  typically *āyur dehi…*, *mahiṣaghni…* and *sarva-maṅgala-maṅgalye…*, all
  of which appear above — and some use a shorter offering refrain (*eṣa
  sacandana-gandha-puṣpa-bilva-patrāñjaliḥ oṁ hrīṁ durgāyai namaḥ*, with
  *hrīṁ*, the Devi's seed-syllable). The paddhati in the purohit's hands,
  not this page, is the authority on the morning itself.
- **Pronunciation.** Bengali recitation softens Sanskrit: *va* sounds as
  *ba*, *ṣa* and *śa* both approach *sha*, and final consonants take a
  vowel's breath. Recite as our purohit does; the Devi has heard every
  accent.
