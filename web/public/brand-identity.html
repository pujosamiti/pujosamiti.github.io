Alpona, drawn the right way

Alpona is white rice-paste line-work on a red or earthen ground — so on this site it lives as thin white SVG strokes on jaba bands: the page header, section dividers, event page mastheads. Motifs: podmo (lotus), kalka (paisley), maach (fish — shubho!), dhaner shish (paddy).

More from the Bengali floor — the motifs that come out on Lakshmi Puja nights, at weddings and at the pandal's threshold, drawn the same way: one white line, no fills but the bindu dots.

Fuller, as the rice paste is laid on at the pandal — drawn for the cultural evenings' flyer (/cultural/flyer/01): filled petals and leaves where the paste is thick, borders built up in layers, and every line through a rice-paste filter so it wavers a little, as a finger-drawn line does (hand on any motif or border, with <HandDrawn /> once on the page).

Rules that keep it tasteful

Red leads, alone. Jaba is the only bold voice on a screen. Its deeper sindoor is only ever a hover; palash, genda and shiuli appear in small doses — a live badge, a chip, a garland motif — never as large fills next to the primary red.
One filled red per view. The page's main action wears the solid jaba. Repeated actions — the download pills — are crimson outlines, and a quiet state (a task still to do) is an inset, never a red fill. Spending is matir earth, not rokto.
Yellow is never text. Genda fails contrast on cream. It carries meaning as a fill behind dark ink (like the active event chip), never as lettering.
Blue and green are workers, not decoration. Durba green means one thing everywhere: success — a paid pledge, a settled claim, money in. Aparajita blue marks selection and sharat sky marks information. None of them ever fills a hero or fights jaba for attention.
Alpona is placed, not sprinkled. Header band, section dividers, event mastheads, festive empty-states. Body screens stay quiet so the accounting tables stay readable.
Warm everywhere. No pure black, no cool greys. Text is kali ink, borders lean cream, the ground is tant cotton and cards are its whiter buti — the whole app sits in the laal-paar shada world.
Type carries both scripts. Noto Serif Bengali for display headings (আমাদের পূজা and “Time Table” alike), Hind Siliguri for UI and body. Both free on Google Fonts. This preview approximates them with system faces.
Dark mode is dhunuchi night. Smoky deep brown, brightened jaba and genda, cream ink — festive at night, not a generic grey inversion. At night jaba lightens and the words on its buttons turn dark.

How the red and the white were chosen

Adopted on 27 September 2026, replacing jaba #D70000 on pure white. The reds below were measured against the saris themselves and against a tant-cotton ground of #E8E4DD. It still works as a workbench: tap any red, and any ground, to dress the phone on the right; the one on the left is the palette as it was before.

Chosen: #C40039 on a #E8E4DD ground, with cards in #FAF8F4. Sharat #2A6493, durba #17664F, matir #8A4A30, and panna #2BF5A2 for the one neon. #C40039 is the deepest of the five reds and the closest to the woven cloth (hue 343°; the saris photograph at 345–350°). One red serves everywhere: white on it measures 6.17, and as text it passes on the tant (4.87) and on cards (5.81), so no second shade is needed for small type.

The one neon

The উমা refresh button is the only colour allowed off the pujo palette, so it is found at a glance. A neon is found by its glow, not its lightness, so every option sits near 1:1 against tant; the dark icon inside carries the contrast. The test that separates them: tick the colour-blind box above. Yellow-greens turn the same yellow as the genda Quiz tab beside them. Emerald does not — and it sits opposite #C40039 on the colour wheel, the glowing sibling of paan.
